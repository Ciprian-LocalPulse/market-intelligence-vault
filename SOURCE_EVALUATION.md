# Source evaluation

Source tier is a starting point. Score reliability against the exact claim, access to the underlying
method, provenance, incentives and correction history. A high-tier publisher can still have weak
evidence for a specific inference.

| Tier | Typical origin | Appropriate use | Reliability guidance (0–25) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Government, regulator, audited filing, official disclosure, peer-reviewed study | Defined official measurement or scoped disclosure | 20–25 after method and scope review |
| 2 | Research institution, financial media, association, documented database | Comparable category analysis and corroboration | 16–22 with transparent underlying method |
| 3 | Company website, professional or trade publication | Product claims, listed terms and trade observations | 11–20 depending on directness and incentives |
| 4 | Review, forum, social or community discussion | Existence of sentiment and emerging signals | 5–15; not prevalence without a valid sampling frame |
| 5 | Unknown or unverifiable origin | Research lead only | 0–5; verification required |

Score 0 when there is no source. Score 5 for traceable but unverifiable origin; 10 for a known
origin with opaque methods; 15 for documented methods with material bias or scope limits; 20 for
primary, inspectable evidence with minor limitations; 25 for independently auditable measurement
with precise scope. Intermediate points require notes.

An official disclosure is Tier 1 for the disclosed statement; a marketing assertion on a company
site is generally Tier 3 and does not establish external performance. Lower-tier observations can
reveal a pain or weak signal. Promote them only after independent verification, behavioral testing
and sampling review. Record source ownership in `origin_group` to avoid false corroboration.
