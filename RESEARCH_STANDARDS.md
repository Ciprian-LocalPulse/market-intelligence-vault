# Research standards

## Claim types

| Label | Meaning | Minimum documentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FACT | Scoped directly observed statement | Exact source, date, locator, population and units |
| INFERENCE | Interpretation from observations | Evidence chain, alternative explanation and limitation |
| ESTIMATE | Calculated or modeled quantity | Formula, assumptions, units, range and sensitivity |
| HYPOTHESIS | Proposition requiring a test | Falsification criterion, method, owner and due date |
| RECOMMENDATION | Proposed choice | Supporting claims, risk, upside and validation gate |

Never convert a hypothesis into a fact through repetition. Never hide absent evidence or
generate a source that was not accessed. Mark estimates in the sentence containing the number.
Separate publication date from access date. Preserve quote wording and provide a precise locator.

## Collection protocol

Write the question and exclusions first. Seek supporting and disconfirming observations. Record
search terms, time window, languages, population, inaccessible sources and stopping rule in the
research notes. Retain negative results and explain coverage limits. Avoid treating a sponsored
article, press release reprint and original release as three independent observations.

For customer research, record recruitment, method, sample size, observed behavior, consent and
bias. Minimize identifying information. Report qualitative patterns as patterns, not population
rates. Confirm price sensitivity through behavior where possible.

## Acceptance

A material claim must have a claim ID, typed conclusion, support map, contradiction review,
scope match, confidence, freshness and action. Unsupported claims may remain as research questions,
but cannot be approved as facts or support a capital commitment. Use the QA hard gates.
